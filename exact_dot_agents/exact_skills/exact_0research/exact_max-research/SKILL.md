# Max Deep Research

## Core Philosophy

**NEVER STOP THINKING** — An answer is not the end, it's the start of the next question

**KEEP ASKING WHY** — Question every piece of information

**NATURAL CURIOSITY** — Questions must arise naturally, not from a preset checklist

## Multi-Source Verification

Every key finding must be verified across multiple sources:

| Source | Examples | Tips |
|-------------|----------|---------|
| Primary | Laws, official docs | Maybe truth |
| Community | Reddit, discussions | Real experience |
| Academic | Polymarket, papers, research | Useful analysis |
| Secondary | News, analysis, commentary | Don't be deceived by news |

**Minimum**: 3 independent sources for any critical claim

**Cross-check**: If sources disagree, investigate why

## Multi-Language Research

For topics with international or non-English contexts:

### English (Primary)
- Default for most research
 -Offers a different perspective
- Broadest coverage

### Chinese (When Relevant)
- Information quality may be poor

_If relates to local information, use the corresponding language._

**Process**:
1. Start in English for broad coverage
2. Add Chinese/other languages for local depth
3. Compare findings across languages
4. Note discrepancies and investigate

## Question Patterns

### The "Why" Chain

Every piece of information triggers questions:

| Discovery | Natural Questions |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Date/Time | Why this date? Who decided? Can it change? |
| Number/Percentage | Why this number? Source? Reliable? |
| Rule/Mechanism | Why designed this way? Historical reason? |
| Person/Entity | Who are they? Stake? Why involved? |
| Status | Current state? Why? Will it change? |

### Coverage Dimensions

From one core question, expand to:

1. **Legal/Regulatory** — What's the legal basis?
2. **Historical** — Why did it become this way? Origins?
3. **Mechanics** — How does it work?
4. **Stakeholders** — Who benefits? Who loses?
5. **Current State** — What's happening now?
6. **Future/Change** — Will it change? Who's pushing?

## Example: Texas Senate Research

**Starting Point**: Why does the market extend to May 26?

```
Q1: Why May 26?
    → Found: Texas law sets runoff on 4th Tuesday in May
    → Q2: Why does law say this date?
        → Found: Texas Election Code Sec 41.007
        → Q3: When was this law passed?
            → Found: 1986 legislation, effective 1988
            → Q4: Why change in 1986?
                → Found: Align with Super Tuesday
                → Q5: What's Super Tuesday? Why align?
                    → Found: 1988 Southern Democratic governors' strategy
                    → Q6: Did the strategy work?
                        → Found: Failed — Gore and Jackson split votes
    → Q7: Why does runoff exist at all?
        → Found: Requires 50%+1 majority
        → Q8: Why not plurality?
            → Found: Jim Crow era system
            → Q9: What was the design purpose?
                → Found: Suppress Black voting power
                → Q10: Does this still affect elections today?
                    → Found: Yes — Texas Senate runoff is a direct result
```

**Search Stats**: 35+ searches
**Time Span**: 1903 → 2026
**Coverage**: Law, history, politics, polling, prediction markets
**Languages**: English (primary), verified across multiple US news sources

## Output Report Requirements

### Structure (Adapt to Content)

1. **Core Question** — What the user is asking
2. **Answer Chain** — How each question led to the next
3. **Key Findings** — Root cause analysis results
4. **Impact Analysis** — What it means for the user
5. **Unresolved** — If questions remain, state them clearly

The report should be kept in my Blog Notebook.
It should be long enough to take AT LEAST 20 minutes to read, it's best if it's very very long.

### Quality Standards

- Never pretend to understand
- Conflicting information noted and investigated
- Every key claim has a source
- Uncertain areas marked as "uncertain"
